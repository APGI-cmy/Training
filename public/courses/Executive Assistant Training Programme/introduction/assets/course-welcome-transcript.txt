Executive Assistant — Professional Support and Protective Coordination

01. Your course and its purpose
Welcome to Executive Assistant — Professional Support and Protective Coordination. This course will help you organise executive-support work, prepare reliable information and coordinate the people whose decisions make an arrangement workable. Your learning will include ordinary administrative work as well as situations in which plans change or assistance is needed. This introduction explains the whole course and your learning space before you begin Learning Unit One.

02. Your role connects people and responsibilities
An executive assistant works within a connected system. The executive makes decisions within their authority. Finance, operations and other specialists remain responsible for their own decisions. The executive protection officer and independent control room also have defined responsibilities. Your role is to organise dependable support, make missing information visible and obtain the decisions or acceptance that an arrangement requires. This course does not give you specialist powers or replace your workplace mandate.

03. Your foundations develop in Learning Units One to Four
The course framework brings together thirteen learning units. Your first four units establish the foundations. You will explore the executive partnership and working agreement, professional boundaries, realistic programme planning, and clear communication and records. These topics belong together. A well-organised diary still needs appropriate authority, useful preparation and reliable information. You will learn to explain what is known, what remains uncertain and whose decision is needed.

04. Your coordination develops in Learning Units Five to Nine
The next five units apply those foundations to meetings and events, travel, business administration, protective coordination and the daily coordination cycle. You will work with arrangements that depend on several people. A booking does not prove readiness, and a message being sent does not prove that an assignment has been accepted. Your records will help you keep responsibilities, changes and unfinished work visible.

05. Your judgement develops through Learning Units Ten to Thirteen
The final four units address privacy and access, emergency readiness and assistance, family liaison and wellbeing, and integrated practice and improvement. You will bring routine support and disruption management together. The emphasis remains on factual information, prompt appropriate help, professional boundaries and accepted continuity. Your final integrated work will show how you connect decisions and responsibilities, not simply how many isolated questions you can answer.

06. Your learning moves from explanation to application
Each learning event will help you understand an idea before you are asked to use it. You will hear an explanation, explore a worked example and then practise with a situation that requires your own reasoning. Fictional Meridian examples demonstrate the method. Separate practice situations help you transfer that method rather than copy an answer. Your comparisons and reflections help you recognise what you would improve.

07. You control the scenes and narration
The Previous scene and Next scene controls move you through one scene at a time. The contents menu helps you return to a topic. You can pause or replay the narration. A play symbol beside a card or answer option lets you hear that particular explanation. Listening to an answer option does not select it as your answer; you make your choice using the separate answer control.

08. Your reading and language support remain available
Source reading opens the workbook as an electronic book. Word help explains unfamiliar terms. You can also select an underlined term in the teaching text to open its meaning. Tools and examples contains the working documents that you will learn to prepare, together with completed fictional examples. You do not need to download and import separate progress files.

09. Your practice and assessment serve different purposes
Learning activities let you explore ideas, make mistakes and compare your reasoning with guidance. Your practice is different from a formal assessment. Before a knowledge check, read the stated pass mark, attempt limits and feedback rules. A reviewed tick does not prove competence, and a prepared draft is not an assessor's decision. Follow the assessment instructions provided for your course.

10. You are ready to begin Learning Unit One
Your progress and practice responses save while you work. If a save warning appears, keep the page open and seek help. We wish you the best as you complete the course, and we trust that it will be a valuable experience in your learning and development journey. When you are ready, select Begin Learning Unit One.
