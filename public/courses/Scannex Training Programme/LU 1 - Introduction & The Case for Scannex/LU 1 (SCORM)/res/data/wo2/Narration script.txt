SUMMARY
Why Scannex Is Needed. Summary. Scannex supports a fast, non-intrusive and recorded process. It helps the operator make a fair decision from evidence, while reducing the opportunity for diamond theft.

STATE-01
Diamonds are easy to hide. Diamonds create a unique security problem. A single stone can be extremely small, but still very valuable. Because of their size and value, diamonds can be hidden in places that are very difficult for normal security checks to detect.

A diamond can be concealed in the hand, in clothing, inside a shoe, inside a tool, in food, or even inside the body. This makes diamond theft different from many other forms of theft. The object being stolen may be smaller than a grain of rice, but the financial impact can be enormous.

For the Scannex operator, the key lesson is simple: do not judge the risk by the size of the item. A very small item can create a very large loss.

Key takeaway: Small size does not mean low risk or low value. Why this matters: Small size does not mean low risk or low value. A small stone is shown beside a hand and examples of everyday concealment locations. Its size can conceal its significance.

STATE-02
Metal detectors cannot see diamonds. One of the most important facts for every Scannex operator to understand is this: a diamond is not metal. A diamond is pure carbon. It does not trigger a metal detector.

This means a person could walk through a metal detector with a valuable diamond and the alarm may not sound. The detector is not broken; it is simply looking for the wrong thing.

Metal detectors are useful for detecting metallic objects, but diamonds require a different approach. Scannex uses imaging technology that responds to density, not metal content. That is why low-dose X-ray scanning is so important in a diamond security environment.

Key takeaway: A working metal detector is not sufficient to identify a non-metallic diamond. Why this matters: A working metal detector is not sufficient to identify a non-metallic diamond. The conceptual metal-detector comparison distinguishes metal keys from non-metallic carbon. This is an ordinary control, not the Scannex scanner.

STATE-03
Physical searches have serious limits. Physical searches may deter casual theft, but they cannot reliably stop a determined thief. They are also invasive, slow, and difficult to apply consistently.

A physical search cannot see inside the human body. It may also miss items hidden in hard-to-search areas, clothing seams, shoes, tools, food, or other everyday objects. When queues build up, searches may become rushed, and quality can drop.

There is also a human weakness: physical searches can be affected by pressure, fear, bribery, or collusion. A searcher may miss something accidentally, or may be influenced to ignore it.

This is why a modern security programme cannot depend on physical searching alone.

Key takeaway: A physical search has practical and consistency limits. Why this matters: A physical search has practical and consistency limits. Compare a physical search with image review: the latter produces an image the trained operator can examine and record. The illustration does not guarantee detection.

STATE-04
Concealment methods are difficult to predict. Recorded diamond-theft cases show a consistent pattern: diamonds are often hidden where physical searches are weakest.

Common concealment methods include swallowing, hiding stones inside body orifices, placing stones inside shoes, clothing seams, tools, food, or personal effects. A security officer cannot always guess where a stone may be hidden.

This is where X-ray scanning becomes powerful. It does not depend on guessing. It looks through soft tissue and many everyday materials, helping the operator identify dense objects that may otherwise remain hidden.

The operator’s task is not to assume guilt. The task is to apply a fair, consistent, technology-driven process to everyone.

Key takeaway: Use evidence and a consistent process instead of guessing from appearance. Why this matters: Use evidence and a consistent process instead of guessing from appearance. The labelled clothing and everyday objects illustrate why appearance alone cannot predict where an item may be hidden.

STATE-05
Scannex creates a fair and defensible process. Scannex helps address the weaknesses of traditional security. It is fast, non-intrusive, consistent, and creates a recorded image that can be reviewed.

This matters because the operator is not there to accuse people. The operator is there to apply a fair process that protects the organisation, the workforce, and honest employees.

When detection is reliable and impartial, the opportunity and temptation to steal are reduced. Stored images also create an audit trail, which supports accountability, training, and evidence.

A scanner is only as good as the person operating it. A well-trained operator turns the technology into a strong deterrent.

Key takeaway: Reviewable evidence supports fair, consistent decisions. Why this matters: Reviewable evidence supports fair, consistent decisions. Conceptual full-body Scannex chamber and operator monitor: a recorded image supports an evidence-based process.
