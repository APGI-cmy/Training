SUMMARY
Low Dose, Professional Discipline. Summary. Safety is a connected system. Engineering controls, procedures, records, quality checks and professional judgement all protect the person being scanned.

STATE-01
Low dose still needs discipline. Scannex uses a very low dose, but every scan must still follow the approved procedure. Safety is never a reason to take shortcuts.

Safety protects people, the legal operation of the programme and public confidence. It is the operator’s first responsibility in day-to-day practice; the licensee retains overall responsibility for the programme.

Key takeaway: A low dose never justifies a shortcut. Why this matters: A low dose never justifies a shortcut. The full-body screening setting and safety shield represent the priority placed on protecting people. Repeated small deviations can weaken a well-designed protection system.

STATE-02
Safety is a system. Safety is created by engineering, approved procedures, records, quality checks and trained people. No single safeguard carries the whole burden.

Key takeaway: Engineering, procedures, records, QA and people work together. Why this matters: Engineering, procedures, records, QA and people work together. The labelled layers connect engineering, procedures, records, QA and the operator. Each layer supports the others. When one element is uncertain, use the approved stop, report and escalation route.

STATE-03
The operator is the human safeguard. The operator applies exclusions, does not bypass controls, reports faults and keeps records accurate. Professional judgement protects every person scanned.

Key takeaway: The operator applies safeguards and escalates concerns. Why this matters: The operator applies safeguards and escalates concerns. An operator checks a record beside the full-body scanner. This illustrates the human safeguard and professional judgement.
