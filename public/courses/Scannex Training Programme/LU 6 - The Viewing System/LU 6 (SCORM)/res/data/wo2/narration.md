## summary — awaiting narration

The Viewing System provides different ways to examine the same X-ray data. Viewing modes, palette, brightness, contrast and zoom change its appearance; they do not change the original scan data.

With the mouse, the left button adjusts brightness, the right adjusts contrast and the middle toggles Toolbar and Pan modes. Left and right together switch the saved palette and Auto Palette. Report an incorrectly configured saved palette to the Supervisor.

F1 returns to Normal. F2 selects Un-sharp Mask, F3 Emboss and F4 High Contrast. F5 switches the reference image on or off, F6 inverts colours, F7 zooms out and F8 zooms in. F10 opens Review login and F11 Overview login.

Escape switches Toolbar and Pan modes. Arrow keys pan in Toolbar mode. Space starts Auto-Pan and Shift+Space reverses or controls it. The / and * keys change contrast; + and − change brightness.

Move between a whole-image overview and finer detail using zoom, pan and Auto-Pan. Compare doubtful areas in more than one mode and zoom level. Use a previous image of the same person when one is available.

Annotate and comment factually where appropriate. Use authorised export, TIFF loading and print functions to preserve the examination. The Viewer does not change station configuration.

Key takeaway: Examine systematically, compare views and preserve a traceable decision.

## state-01 — existing narration retained

More Than a Simple Picture. The Viewing System gives the Viewer several ways to examine the same X-ray data. A faint diamond may be difficult to distinguish from bone, clothing fasteners or the natural texture of the body. Changing the viewing mode, palette, brightness, contrast or zoom can make a doubtful object easier to recognise. A thorough Viewer does not rely on a single view. 

Key takeaway: The image data remains the same—the tools change how clearly the Viewer can examine it. Why this matters: Use the viewing system methodically and make a documented, accountable decision.

## state-02 — existing narration retained

Continuous Control Without Looking Away. The mouse allows the Viewer to adjust the image while keeping their eyes on the area being examined:
Left button: Adjust brightness.
Right button: Adjust contrast.
Middle button: Toggle between Toolbar and Pan modes.
Left and right buttons together: Toggle between the saved palette and Auto Palette. Auto Palette maximises the palette for the area currently visible. If the saved palette has not been configured correctly, the Viewer must report it to the Supervisor. 

Key takeaway: Left controls brightness. Right controls contrast. Middle switches Toolbar and Pan. Why this matters: Use the viewing system methodically and make a documented, accountable decision.

## state-04 — existing narration retained

Navigate and Adjust from the Keyboard. The keyboard can control the image without using the mouse:
Escape: Toggle between Toolbar and Pan modes.
Arrow keys: Pan the image while in Toolbar mode.
Space: Start Auto-Pan.
Shift+Space: Reverse or control Auto-Pan.
/ and *: Decrease or increase contrast.
+ and −: Increase or decrease brightness. Brightness and contrast shortcuts work in both Toolbar and Pan modes. 

Key takeaway: The keyboard allows experienced Viewers to work quickly without taking their attention away from the image. Why this matters: Use the viewing system methodically and make a documented, accountable decision.

## state-05 — existing narration retained

Move from the Whole Image to Fine Detail. Zoom changes the size of the image: Zoom ¼: Wide overview
Zoom ½: Wider view
Zoom 1: Normal or actual size
Zoom 2: Twice normal size
Zoom 4: Close detail

Panning moves the visible window around a zoomed image. In Pan mode, the mouse moves the image directly. In Toolbar mode, the arrow keys move it. Auto-Pan systematically moves the view across the image. 

Key takeaway: Examine doubtful areas at more than one zoom level—without losing awareness of the whole image. Why this matters: Use the viewing system methodically and make a documented, accountable decision.

## state-06 — existing narration retained

Is the Object New or Was It There Before?. Pressing F5 calls up a previous image of the same person, if one exists. Comparing the current scan with a reference image can help the Viewer determine whether a suspicious shape: Has appeared since the earlier scan; Was already present; May be part of the person, such as a medical implant or healed injury. The Reference Image supports judgement; it does not make the decision for the Viewer. 

Key takeaway: A new object may be significant. A familiar object may have an innocent explanation. Why this matters: Use the viewing system methodically and make a documented, accountable decision.

## state-07 — existing narration retained

Record What You Saw. The Viewing System allows the Viewer to preserve evidence of their examination:
Annotate: Place a marker on an area of interest.
Comment: Attach written notes to the image.
Export: Save the current image as a JPG, BMP or TIFF file.
Load Image: Load a stored TIFF image.
Print Screen: Print the current view. Annotations and comments help another authorised person understand what drew the Viewer’s attention. 

Key takeaway: A recorded decision can be reconstructed, reviewed and defended. Why this matters: Use the viewing system methodically and make a documented, accountable decision.
