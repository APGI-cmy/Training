SUMMARY
Explain the Science with Confidence. Summary. Explaining the science accurately supports dignity, confidence and safe cooperation. The operator must be able to state what the scan does, why controls exist and how images are interpreted.

STATE-01
Explain with confidence. Understanding the science helps you explain the scan calmly and accurately to a person who may feel uncertain. Clear explanations build confidence and trust.

Key takeaway: A calm explanation should be accurate and understandable. Why this matters: A calm explanation should be accurate and understandable. An operator explains the scan to a person beside the full-body chamber. The scene illustrates respectful communication.

STATE-02
Follow safety for a reason. Safety procedures are not arbitrary boxes to tick. They control a powerful technology and protect the person being scanned, colleagues and the public.

Key takeaway: Safety procedures protect people and control the use of the technology. Why this matters: Safety procedures protect people and control the use of the technology. The chamber provides the context for controlled screening and safety boundaries.

STATE-03
Read images more accurately. Bright and dark areas are not random. They show how different materials allow X-rays through or block them. Understanding this is the foundation of good interpretation.

Key takeaway: Understanding attenuation helps explain light and dark image areas. Why this matters: Understanding attenuation helps explain light and dark image areas. The conceptual body image illustrates normal anatomy that must be considered when examining light and dark areas.

STATE-04
Use the approved process. Reliable screening depends on the same controlled steps being followed every time, including authorised operation, image review and appropriate escalation.

Key takeaway: Use the approved process consistently and escalate concerns appropriately. Why this matters: Use the approved process consistently and escalate concerns appropriately. STATE-05
Protect the decision. A fair decision comes from evidence, careful interpretation and proper record keeping, not from assumption or appearance.

Key takeaway: Base decisions on evidence, interpretation and proper records. Why this matters: Base decisions on evidence, interpretation and proper records. Two colleagues review a body image. The scene illustrates discussion based on evidence.
