SUMMARY
From Discovery to Modern Screening. Summary. Modern Scannex screening uses a long-established scientific principle together with controlled equipment, dose limits and trained operators.

STATE-01
An unexpected glow. In 1895, Wilhelm Conrad Röntgen noticed that an unseen emission from a covered experimental tube caused a distant fluorescent screen to glow. He later showed that the radiation could pass through material and expose a photographic plate.

Key takeaway: Röntgen discovered X-rays in 1895 while investigating an unexpected glow. Why this matters: Röntgen discovered X-rays in 1895 while investigating an unexpected glow. The fluorescent screen on the right glows in the historical reconstruction. This represents the observation that led to the discovery.

STATE-02
The X-ray. Röntgen called the new radiation X-rays because its nature was initially unknown. The discovery made it possible to see inside materials without opening them.

Key takeaway: The name X-ray reflected the initially unknown nature of the radiation. Why this matters: The name X-ray reflected the initially unknown nature of the radiation. This reconstruction illustrates Röntgen’s early investigation and a hand image. It is not an authentic historical photograph.

STATE-03
Safety lessons. Early users did not understand the risks of overexposure. Their experience led to radiation protection principles, controlled equipment and safer procedures.

Key takeaway: Uncontrolled exposure led to lessons that inform modern radiation protection. Why this matters: Uncontrolled exposure led to lessons that inform modern radiation protection. The early tube and modern full-body chamber contrast the discovery with controlled modern use. The image does not specify a dose.

STATE-04
Precision Scannex. Modern Scannex applies the same principle with a focused beam, sensitive detectors, operating controls and strict dose management.

Key takeaway: Modern imaging combines the scientific principle with approved equipment and controls. Why this matters: Modern imaging combines the scientific principle with approved equipment and controls. The full-body chamber and monitor represent the modern screening context.
