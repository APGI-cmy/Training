# TK-LU05-LA01 — Family readiness in normal life

## Introduction

Family readiness starts with a short, calm conversation. Family members do not need confidential organisational information to know that personal safety comes before a work request, a payment, access to a system or service continuity. This activity helps you agree a few ordinary habits that support each other and protect privacy.

## Agree a shared principle

Agree one clear principle at home: if anyone feels unsafe or receives a request linked to pressure, safety comes first. No family member needs to resolve a work issue, payment, access request or service problem alone. The first job is to stay safe and contact trusted support when it is safe to do so.

## Keep information bounded

Family members can protect one another by sharing personal, household and work information deliberately. An unexpected caller or visitor does not need a travel plan, routine, school detail, job-access detail or confirmation that someone is home. It is reasonable to pause, say that the information cannot be shared and use a known contact to verify a request.

## Identify trusted support

Decide who each person can contact if something feels unusual. Keep the list small and practical. It might include a close adult family member and an approved organisational or community support contact. Do not publish the list or place it in a public profile. Each person only needs the contact details and safety guidance appropriate to them.

## Confirm normal changes

Ordinary plans change. A field visit moves, a lift arrangement changes or a service appointment shifts. Confirm an unexpected change through a contact you already know, rather than a number or link supplied in a new message. This simple habit lets normal life continue while reducing unnecessary uncertainty.

## Summary

Family readiness supports normal life. Agree that safety comes first, keep private information bounded, know who to contact and confirm unexpected changes through established people or services. These habits give family members a calm way to support one another without carrying confidential work information or making private investigations.
