INTRO
Glossary: Key Legal and Human-Rights Terms. Learning outcome: Apply the supplied Scannex legal and human-rights protocol: lawful authority, informed consent, privacy, dignity, neutral selection, proportionate response and authorised escalation. Before the legal and human-rights activities, review all sixteen terms. Select a term to read its meaning, practical purpose and Scannex example. This unit teaches the supplied APGI protocol; follow applicable local law and the current approved site procedure. A learning example does not authorise a scan, search, detention or arrest.

SUMMARY
Glossary: Key Legal and Human-Rights Terms. Summary. Legal basis and informed consent guide the decision to scan. Privacy, lawful retention, dignity, neutral selection and non-discrimination guide the encounter. Proportionality, factual indications, reasonable suspicion, secure evidence, lawful detention, arrest authority, presumption of innocence and judicial review guide any follow-up. Use the terms in practice and escalate uncertainty.

STATE-01
Legal basis. The lawful authority that permits a scan. It comes from the applicable local law and the approved procedure—not simply because an operator wants to scan someone.

Why it matters: It prevents scanning from becoming arbitrary and protects the operator, the person and the operation.

Scannex example: Before a scan, the operator checks that the local procedure authorises Scannex screening in that setting.

Key takeaway: It prevents scanning from becoming arbitrary and protects the operator, the person and the operation. Why this matters: It prevents scanning from becoming arbitrary and protects the operator, the person and the operation. Upper left: the document represents the approved legal authority, beside a full-body chamber.

STATE-02
Informed consent. A person’s voluntary agreement after they have been told what will happen, why it is needed, the approved dose information and radiation-protection arrangements, and what record will be kept.

Why it matters: Consent is meaningful only when the person has enough information to decide. If they are unsure, the correct response is to pause and escalate—not to pressure them.

Scannex example: An operator explains the purpose of the scan and answers questions before asking the person to agree.

Key takeaway: Consent is meaningful only when the person has enough information to decide. If they are unsure, the correct response is to pause and escalate—not to pressure them. Why this matters: Consent is meaningful only when the person has enough information to decide. If they are unsure, the correct response is to pause and escalate—not to pressure them. Upper right: an operator explains the process before asking the person to agree.

STATE-03
Privacy. Keeping personal information, scan images and records protected from unnecessary viewing, sharing or disclosure.

Why it matters: A scan can reveal sensitive information. Protecting privacy maintains trust and supports lawful handling of personal data.

Scannex example: The scan image is viewed only by authorised personnel and is not discussed with colleagues who have no operational need to know.

Key takeaway: A scan can reveal sensitive information. Protecting privacy maintains trust and supports lawful handling of personal data. Why this matters: A scan can reveal sensitive information. Protecting privacy maintains trust and supports lawful handling of personal data. Lower left: the lock beside the display represents authorised access to sensitive images.

STATE-04
Lawful retention. Keeping a scan image or related record only for the approved reason and approved period, then disposing of it securely when that period ends.

Why it matters: Records must support a legitimate process, not become an informal archive that can later be misused.

Scannex example: The operator follows the approved retention schedule rather than keeping an image “just in case.”

Key takeaway: Records must support a legitimate process, not become an informal archive that can later be misused. Why this matters: Records must support a legitimate process, not become an informal archive that can later be misused. Lower right: the locked record and calendar represent secure storage and an approved retention schedule.

STATE-05
Dignity and respect. Treating every person as an individual with worth: calm communication, personal space, decency and no ridicule, intimidation or rough treatment.

Why it matters: Security work is lawful only when it remains humane. Respect also helps people understand the process and cooperate safely.

Scannex example: The operator explains the next step in a calm voice and avoids language or behaviour that could embarrass the person.

Key takeaway: Security work is lawful only when it remains humane. Respect also helps people understand the process and cooperate safely. Why this matters: Security work is lawful only when it remains humane. Respect also helps people understand the process and cooperate safely. Upper left: calm communication and personal space illustrate dignity and respectful treatment.

STATE-06
Same-gender rule. Where a physical search becomes necessary, arranging it with an officer of the same gender in line with local procedure and with appropriate privacy.

Why it matters: This protects dignity and reduces the risk of an intrusive response becoming humiliating or unlawful.

Scannex example: After an indication, the operator pauses the process and requests the appropriate same-gender colleague rather than continuing alone.

Key takeaway: This protects dignity and reduces the risk of an intrusive response becoming humiliating or unlawful. Why this matters: This protects dignity and reduces the risk of an intrusive response becoming humiliating or unlawful. Upper right: the private search setting illustrates arranging the appropriate same-gender officer under the supplied site protocol.

STATE-07
Neutral selection. Following the approved random access-control selection exactly as it is generated. It means not choosing, excluding or altering selection because of appearance, familiarity, status or personal views.

Why it matters: Neutral selection keeps screening fair and defensible. It prevents discrimination and avoids targeting or sparing individuals.

Scannex example: If the approved random system selects a familiar colleague, the operator applies the same process as for anyone else.

Key takeaway: Neutral selection keeps screening fair and defensible. It prevents discrimination and avoids targeting or sparing individuals. Why this matters: Neutral selection keeps screening fair and defensible. It prevents discrimination and avoids targeting or sparing individuals. Lower left: the selection symbol and group illustrate applying the approved neutral selection process.

STATE-08
Non-discrimination. Applying the same approved rules fairly, without treating people differently because of race, sex, religion, nationality, appearance, relationship or another personal characteristic.

Why it matters: Fair and consistent treatment is a human-rights obligation and protects the credibility of the operation.

Scannex example: Two visitors who meet the same selection criteria receive the same explanation and procedure, regardless of who they are.

Key takeaway: Fair and consistent treatment is a human-rights obligation and protects the credibility of the operation. Why this matters: Fair and consistent treatment is a human-rights obligation and protects the credibility of the operation. Lower right: the people shown receive the same approved rules without personal favouritism.

STATE-09
Proportionality. Using the least intrusive effective response that is justified by the security purpose. The response must go no further than necessary.

Why it matters: A security concern does not automatically justify the most intrusive action. Proportionality protects rights while allowing the operation to manage genuine risk.

Scannex example: If clarification can be achieved through the authorised scan process, the operator does not immediately move to a more intrusive measure.

Key takeaway: A security concern does not automatically justify the most intrusive action. Proportionality protects rights while allowing the operation to manage genuine risk. Why this matters: A security concern does not automatically justify the most intrusive action. Proportionality protects rights while allowing the operation to manage genuine risk. Upper left: the balance illustrates choosing a response that goes no further than the lawful purpose requires.

STATE-10
Positive indication. An image or result that may require further attention. It is an indication, not proof that a person has done something wrong.

Why it matters: This distinction prevents accusation, humiliation and premature punishment. The operator must remain calm, factual and lawful.

Scannex example: An operator notes a possible item on the image, secures the record and follows the local escalation process without accusing the person.

Key takeaway: This distinction prevents accusation, humiliation and premature punishment. The operator must remain calm, factual and lawful. Why this matters: This distinction prevents accusation, humiliation and premature punishment. The operator must remain calm, factual and lawful. Upper right: the indicated image feature requires factual review; the scene does not prove an offence.

STATE-11
Reasonable suspicion. A lawful, objective basis for a next step, founded on documented facts and the approved local procedure—not intuition, prejudice or a guess.

Why it matters: It ensures that escalation is justified and can be explained afterwards.

Scannex example: A supervisor reviews the image, the relevant facts and the local threshold before authorising any additional action.

Key takeaway: It ensures that escalation is justified and can be explained afterwards. Why this matters: It ensures that escalation is justified and can be explained afterwards. Lower left: documented facts and a supervisor’s review support the lawful next step.

STATE-12
Preserve evidence. Protecting the image, record and any relevant item so that they remain accurate, secure and available for the authorised process.

Why it matters: Good evidence handling protects both the investigation and the person from mistakes, contamination or later dispute.

Scannex example: The operator follows the approved chain of custody and records who received the image or item.

Key takeaway: Good evidence handling protects both the investigation and the person from mistakes, contamination or later dispute. Why this matters: Good evidence handling protects both the investigation and the person from mistakes, contamination or later dispute. Lower right: the protected record and handover illustrate maintaining evidence integrity and custody.

STATE-13
Detention. A situation where a person is not free to leave. It is not a punishment and it does not mean that the person has been found guilty.

Why it matters: A detained person retains fundamental rights and must be treated humanely throughout.

Scannex example: If local law allows temporary detention, the person is held only in an officially recognised setting and is told the reason.

Key takeaway: A detained person retains fundamental rights and must be treated humanely throughout. Why this matters: A detained person retains fundamental rights and must be treated humanely throughout. Upper left: a person in an authorised holding setting retains rights and must be told the reason.

STATE-14
Arrest. A formal legal action that places a person in custody under the authority and process set by local law.

Why it matters: Arrest has a higher legal threshold and procedure than an initial indication or temporary detention. Operators must not treat the terms as interchangeable.

Scannex example: A Scannex operator reports the facts through the approved chain; police or authorised officers make arrest decisions where the law requires it.

Key takeaway: Arrest has a higher legal threshold and procedure than an initial indication or temporary detention. Operators must not treat the terms as interchangeable. Why this matters: Arrest has a higher legal threshold and procedure than an initial indication or temporary detention. Operators must not treat the terms as interchangeable. Upper right: the authorised officer represents a formal arrest decision under applicable local law.

STATE-15
Presumption of innocence. The principle that a person is not treated as guilty merely because they are selected, scanned, detained or associated with an indication.

Why it matters: It shapes language, behaviour and decisions: investigation must remain fair, factual and respectful.

Scannex example: The operator says “we need to follow the approved process” rather than making a statement that assumes wrongdoing.

Key takeaway: It shapes language, behaviour and decisions: investigation must remain fair, factual and respectful. Why this matters: It shapes language, behaviour and decisions: investigation must remain fair, factual and respectful. Lower left: respectful communication reflects the presumption of innocence during the process.

STATE-16
Judicial review. Independent legal oversight of whether detention or other restrictions on liberty are lawful and properly justified.

Why it matters: It provides a safeguard against arbitrary or prolonged restriction of a person’s freedom.

Scannex example: The records are kept accurately so an authorised court or reviewer can assess whether the process was lawful.

Key takeaway: It provides a safeguard against arbitrary or prolonged restriction of a person’s freedom. Why this matters: It provides a safeguard against arbitrary or prolonged restriction of a person’s freedom. Lower right: the legal reviewer and record represent independent oversight of the restriction of liberty.
