INTRO
Rights During Detention. Learning outcome: Apply the supplied Scannex legal and human-rights protocol: lawful authority, informed consent, privacy, dignity, neutral selection, proportionate response and authorised escalation. This activity explains rights during detention. Select each labelled control, read the complete narrative, enlarge the highlighted illustration and review the summary before the knowledge check. Apply the supplied protocol through the current approved local procedure.

SUMMARY
Rights During Detention. Summary. Use calm, factual communication and maintain humane conditions. An indication does not cancel fair treatment. Do not obstruct authorised communication. These needs are rights, not optional comforts. Escalate promptly when specialised support or protection is needed.

STATE-01
Humanity and dignity. A person deprived of liberty must be treated with respect. There must be no threats, violence, degrading treatment, coercion or humiliation. Detention is not a punishment.

Key takeaway: Use calm, factual communication and maintain humane conditions. Why this matters: Use calm, factual communication and maintain humane conditions. Upper left: calm communication in an authorised setting illustrates humanity and dignity.

STATE-02
Fair treatment. The person is presumed innocent. They should be told the reason for detention and any charge must be explained through the lawful process.

Key takeaway: An indication does not cancel fair treatment. Why this matters: An indication does not cancel fair treatment. Upper right: the rights explanation and record illustrate fair treatment and informing the person.

STATE-03
Information and contact. Family and legal representatives must receive information in line with the applicable procedure. Private legal contact is protected.

Key takeaway: Do not obstruct authorised communication. Why this matters: Do not obstruct authorised communication. Lower left: the telephone and legal representative illustrate permitted family and private legal contact.

STATE-04
Humane conditions. A person in detention needs an officially recognised place, food, water, health care, hygiene, shelter, clothing and appropriate exercise.

Key takeaway: These needs are rights, not optional comforts. Why this matters: These needs are rights, not optional comforts. Lower right: food, water, a bed, hygiene and medical care illustrate humane conditions.

STATE-05
Special protections. Respect religious and moral beliefs. Apply the additional safeguards required for women and juveniles. Judicial review remains a right.

Key takeaway: Escalate promptly when specialised support or protection is needed. Why this matters: Escalate promptly when specialised support or protection is needed. Lower-right corner: the woman, juvenile and shield symbols illustrate additional protections; use the applicable procedure and specialised support.
