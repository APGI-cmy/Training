INTRO
Digestive Route and Peristalsis. Learning outcome: Trace a swallowed object’s route and use movement and anatomical position as interpretation clues. A swallowed object follows a predictable route through the digestive tract. Understanding this route helps the operator determine whether an apparent object is located where swallowed contents could reasonably be found. Explore all six stages, then the peristalsis note and gallbladder caution.

SUMMARY
Key takeaway: The route is mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine, large intestine, then rectum and anus. Peristalsis is travelling muscular contractions that move contents through the tract and can change a swallowed object’s position. Where a reference image is available, compare the same region and account for body position. The gallbladder is outside this route, and gallstones can resemble dense objects. Movement or apparent density alone does not establish what a feature is.

STATE-01
Stage 1: Mouth. A swallowed diamond enters through the mouth. Diamonds may also be concealed behind the lips, underneath the tongue or inside the cheeks without being swallowed. Mouth region: the entry to the swallow route. Concealment can also occur in the lips, under the tongue or in the cheeks.

STATE-02
Stage 2: Oesophagus. After swallowing, the object passes down the oesophagus towards the stomach.

The operator should recognise the oesophagus as part of the continuous digestive route. The gold passage marks the oesophagus between the throat and stomach.

STATE-03
Stage 3: Stomach. The object enters the stomach and may remain there temporarily before moving further through the digestive tract.

Its position should be evaluated against the expected location of the stomach. The gold organ is the stomach, located beyond the oesophagus.

STATE-04
Stage 4: Small Intestine. The object moves laterally through the twisting loops of the small intestine.

Its position may change considerably as digestive contents move. The gold twisting loops are the small intestine.

STATE-05
Stage 5: Large Intestine. The object travels through the large intestine or colon. The route passes upward and then downward through the abdomen. The gold outer loop is the large intestine, also called the colon.

STATE-06
Stage 6: Rectum and Anus. The object reaches the rectum and is eventually expelled through the anus.

The anus is also frequently used as a direct concealment site. The highlighted lower passage illustrates the end of the route: rectum and anus.

STATE-07
Timeline Interpretation Note: Peristalsis. Peristalsis consists of travelling muscular contractions. It moves swallowed contents through the digestive tract.

Where a reference image is available, a feature that has changed position along this route supports the possibility of swallowed contents.

A feature that remains fixed is not moving through the tract: first account for normal anatomy or an external object, then apply the approved, lawful and dignified response.

Key takeaway: Compare like-for-like. Movement is a clue to possible swallowed contents; a single comparison does not establish the object’s identity. Peristalsis is muscular movement along the tract; this comparison diagram is conceptual.

STATE-08
Gallbladder Caution. The gallbladder is not part of the route followed by swallowed contents.

Gallstones may nevertheless resemble diamonds. The operator must know the gallbladder’s normal position and avoid automatically treating every stone-like feature as concealed contraband.

Unlike skeletal structures, gallstones may appear isolated rather than patterned. Confirm that the feature lies in the expected gallbladder region and compare it with any available reference image before treating it as suspicious. The circle identifies the gallbladder region. It marks anatomical location; it does not prove that a visible density is a gallstone.
