# TK-LU05-LA02 — Trusted support plan

## Introduction

A trusted-support plan should be small enough to use. It gives family members a way to reach the right people, confirm an unexpected change and communicate what happened in plain language. The plan does not need detailed organisational information or public contact lists. It needs reliable people and a shared understanding of how to ask for help.

## Choose reliable contacts

Choose a few people who are calm, reachable and able to help a family member decide what to do next. Agree the list together and review it when circumstances change. A young adult family member may need different contacts from a person who travels for work. The important point is that each person knows a trusted route before an uncomfortable moment occurs.

## Keep channels private

Keep a support concern within trusted, approved communication channels. Avoid posting it in a community group, forwarding it widely or asking social media to identify a person. Public sharing can expose private details and make a fair assessment harder. A known phone number or approved work route is more useful than a number supplied in an unexpected message.

## Confirm an unexpected change

If a service appointment, travel arrangement or work visit changes unexpectedly, confirm it through an established person or provider. Use a saved, known contact route. Do not rely on a new link, a number in an unexpected message or a claim that the change must remain secret. Verification is a routine courtesy that protects everyone involved.

## Use calm factual language

When asking for help, describe what happened without guessing why it happened. Say what you heard, saw or received, when it occurred and whether you feel safe. For example, a caller asked about a routine and said the answer was urgent. Clear facts allow a trusted person or trained team to assess the concern without turning it into an accusation.

## Summary

A useful support plan is private, simple and current. Choose reliable contacts, keep the conversation inside trusted channels, confirm unexpected changes through known routes and communicate facts calmly. The plan gives families a way to ask for support while protecting both dignity and privacy.
