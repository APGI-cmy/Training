SUMMARY
Key takeaway: Check the digestive route and any available evidence of movement. Account for known skeletal anatomy, repeating patterns and symmetry. Confirm expected anatomical position, including the gallbladder region. Compare mottled bone texture with a uniform dense appearance. A feature that remains unexplained is an anomaly. Resolve it through the approved, lawful and dignified response chain.

STATE-01
Check the digestive route. Is the feature on the expected path from mouth to anus? A plausible route guides review but does not identify the contents.

STATE-02
Check for movement. Where reference imaging is available, has the feature changed position? Use movement as one clue alongside anatomy, pattern and texture.

STATE-03
Check for known skeletal anatomy. Could it be a spinous process, pedicle, atlas, carpal bone, tarsal bone or patella? Known anatomical structures should be accounted for before an anomaly is assessed.

STATE-04
Check pattern and symmetry. Does the feature repeat, occur in a pair or mirror the opposite limb? Repetition and symmetry support an anatomical explanation.

STATE-05
Check its expected position. Does it sit where normal anatomy, including the gallbladder region, would be expected? Location is an interpretation clue; it is not a medical diagnosis.

STATE-06
Check texture and density. Does the feature show the mottled internal texture and softer edges commonly associated with bone, or is it uniformly dense with sharper edges? STATE-07
Resolve what remains unexplained. Once normal anatomy has been accounted for, assess the remaining anomaly through the approved, lawful and dignified process. An anomaly is an unexplained feature, not an automatic finding of contraband.
