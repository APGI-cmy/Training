# TK-LU05-LA03 — Visitors and service providers

## Introduction

Visitor readiness supports ordinary hospitality and professional service. It does not require family members to distrust people who work in or visit their community. It asks households to prepare expected appointments, verify an unexpected change through a known route and avoid sharing private family or work details unnecessarily.

## Prepare an expected visit

When a visit is planned, agree the broad purpose and who expects the person. Keep routine details private. A household does not need to publicise an appointment, share a work role or provide personal schedules to make a normal visit run smoothly. Preparation helps everyone know what is expected without treating the visitor as a problem.

## Verify a change respectfully

An unexpected change does not prove that anything is wrong. If an appointment moves, a person arrives early or a new contact communicates on behalf of a provider, confirm the change through an established provider contact when you can. Keep the interaction respectful and do not share more personal or work information while you verify.

## Brief household support fairly

Household support staff and adult family members deserve a clear, respectful briefing. Explain that callers and visitors should not receive private information about routines, travel or work, and that any unusual request can be referred to the person who arranged the service. This is a shared privacy rule, not a test of anyone’s loyalty.

## Protect material and routines

Before an appointment, put work material, access devices and household documents away. Avoid discussing a work role, travel schedule or family routine where it can be overheard. These everyday boundaries protect privacy and let everyone focus on the purpose of the visit.

## Summary

Good visitor discipline remains courteous. Prepare expected visits, verify a change through a known route, brief household support with respect and keep work material and private routines out of view and conversation. These habits protect normal life while treating every visitor fairly.
