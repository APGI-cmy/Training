STATE-01
The language of dose. Equivalent and effective radiation dose are expressed in sieverts. In practical radiation protection, smaller units are commonly used: millisieverts and microsieverts. One sievert equals one thousand millisieverts, and one millisievert equals one thousand microsieverts. Use the dose quantity specified in the approved records.

Key takeaway: Equivalent and effective dose use the sievert and its smaller units. Why this matters: Equivalent and effective dose use the sievert and its smaller units. The three unit symbols are used as a reminder of sievert, millisievert and microsievert. Their sizes are illustrative, not a dose comparison.

STATE-02
International framework. The ICRP provides the international scientific framework for radiation protection. National authorities apply and enforce relevant requirements locally.

Key takeaway: Scientific recommendations and local regulatory requirements have different roles. Why this matters: Scientific recommendations and local regulatory requirements have different roles. Dose in familiar context. A dose comparison must use a verified Scannex scan value, the same dose quantity and a stated reference. Medical X-ray doses depend on the examination and technique. Flight exposure depends on factors such as route and duration. Use approved information and do not describe a scan as equivalent to a particular flight without that basis.
